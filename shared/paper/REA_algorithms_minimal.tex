\section{Problem and notation}

Let $c$ be a fixed prompt and let
\[
X=X_{1:T}=(X_1,\ldots,X_T)
\]
be a completion of fixed length $T$. The language model defines
\[
\pM(x\mid c)
=
\prod_{t=1}^{T}\pM(x_t\mid c,x_{1:t-1}).
\]
Conditioning on $c$ is suppressed below.

Let $\phi_{\rm raw}(x)$ be the observable of interest, which in our
experiments is the Automated Readability Index (ARI). Sampling may be biased
using either the raw observable or its capped version
\[
\psi_C(x)=\min\{\phi_{\rm raw}(x),C\}.
\]
The cap changes the sampling distribution, but not the event whose probability
is estimated. For a threshold $a$, define
\[
f_a(x)=\1\{\phi_{\rm raw}(x)\geq a\}.
\]
Our target is
\[
\bar f=\E_{\pM}[f(X)].
\]
For $f=f_a$, this is the base-model tail probability
\[
\bar f_a
=
\mathbb P_{\pM}\!\left(\phi_{\rm raw}(X)\geq a\right).
\]

For a bias $\lambda$ and a sampling potential $\psi$, define
\[
\gamma_\lambda(x)=\pM(x)e^{-\lambda\psi(x)},
\qquad
Z_\lambda=\sum_x\gamma_\lambda(x),
\qquad
\pi_\lambda(x)=\frac{\gamma_\lambda(x)}{Z_\lambda}.
\]
Negative values of $\lambda$ favour large values of $\psi$. At $\lambda=0$,
$\pi_0=\pM$ and $Z_0=1$.

\section{Original algorithm: annealed TPS followed by MBAR}

The original method first generates samples from several tilted distributions
with annealed Transition Path Sampling (TPS). It then combines the samples
using normalising constants obtained by MBAR.

\subsection{Annealed TPS}

Choose biases
\[
0=\lambda_0>\lambda_1>\cdots>\lambda_K
\]
and initialise a completion with $x\sim\pM$. At bias $\lambda_k$, one TPS
transition is:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
    \item Choose uniformly a suffix length $m\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$.

    \item Preserve the first $T-m$ completion tokens. Writing
    \[
    x=(u,x^{\rm s}),
    \]
    generate a proposed suffix from the base model,
    \[
    y^{\rm s}\sim\pM(\,\cdot\mid c,u),
    \qquad
    y=(u,y^{\rm s}).
    \]

    \item Accept $y$ with probability
    \[
    \boxed{
    A_k^{\rm TPS}(x,y)
    =
    \min\left\{
    1,
    e^{-\lambda_k[\psi(y)-\psi(x)]}
    \right\}.}
    \]
    If the proposal is rejected, retain $x$.
\end{enumerate}

The transition is repeated at every bias. The final state at $\lambda_k$ is
used to initialise the chain at $\lambda_{k+1}$, and the resulting states are
stored as samples from the corresponding tilted distributions.

The acceptance probability follows from the Metropolis--Hastings ratio. Since
$x$ and $y$ share the prefix $u$,
\[
\pM(x)=\pM(u)\pM(x^{\rm s}\mid c,u),
\qquad
\pM(y)=\pM(u)\pM(y^{\rm s}\mid c,u).
\]
The language-model and proposal probabilities cancel, leaving
\[
\frac{
\pi_{\lambda_k}(y)q_m(x\mid y)
}{
\pi_{\lambda_k}(x)q_m(y\mid x)
}
=
e^{-\lambda_k[\psi(y)-\psi(x)]}.
\]

\subsection{Umbrella sampling and MBAR}

For each bias $\lambda$, the normalising constant of the tilted distribution
is
\begin{align*}
Z(\lambda)
&=
\sum_x \pM(x)e^{-\lambda\psi(x)}\\
&=
\E_{\pM}\!\left[e^{-\lambda\psi(X)}\right].
\end{align*}
This quantity is generally unknown.

Choose $K$ tilted distributions $\pi_{\lambda_1},\ldots,
\pi_{\lambda_K}$ and coefficients $\alpha_k>0$ such that
$\sum_k\alpha_k=1$. Umbrella sampling introduces the mixture
\[
p_{\rm Mix}(x)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_k\pi_{\lambda_k}(x).
\]
Substituting the definition of each tilted distribution gives
\[
\boxed{
p_{\rm Mix}(x)
=
\pM(x)
\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\alpha_k Z(\lambda_k)^{-1}e^{-\lambda_k\psi(x)}.}
\]
Therefore the importance weight from the mixture back to the base model is
\[
\boxed{
w_{\rm Mix}(x)
=
\frac{\pM(x)}{p_{\rm Mix}(x)}
=
\left[
\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\alpha_k Z(\lambda_k)^{-1}e^{-\lambda_k\psi(x)}
\right]^{-1}.}
\]
The factor $\pM(x)$ cancels, but the weight still depends on all the unknown
normalising constants.

For any test function $f$, the umbrella-sampling identity is
\begin{align*}
\bar f
&=
\E_{p_{\rm Mix}}\!\left[w_{\rm Mix}(X)f(X)\right]\\
&=
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_k
\E_{\pi_{\lambda_k}}\!\left[
w_{\rm Mix}(X)f(X)
\right].
\end{align*}
Taking $f(x)=e^{-\lambda_j\psi(x)}$ gives one equation for every partition
function:
\[
\boxed{
Z(\lambda_j)
=
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_k
\E_{\pi_{\lambda_k}}\!\left[
w_{\rm Mix}(X)e^{-\lambda_j\psi(X)}
\right],
\qquad j=1,\ldots,K.}
\]
Because $w_{\rm Mix}$ depends on every $Z(\lambda_k)$, these equations are
solved jointly. This self-consistent umbrella-sampling estimator is MBAR.

If $N_k$ samples $x_{k,1},\ldots,x_{k,N_k}$ are retained from component $k$,
the expectation of interest is estimated by
\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\bar f}_{\rm TPS\text{-}MBAR}
=
\frac{
\displaystyle
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\alpha_k}{N_k}
\sum_{i=1}^{N_k}
\widehat w_{\rm Mix}(x_{k,i})f(x_{k,i})
}{
\displaystyle
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\alpha_k}{N_k}
\sum_{i=1}^{N_k}
\widehat w_{\rm Mix}(x_{k,i})
}.}
\]
With the empirical mixture, $\alpha_k=N_k/N_{\rm tot}$, where
$N_{\rm tot}=\sum_kN_k$. The samples from all tilted distributions are thus
combined through one common mixture weight and one final ratio.

\section{Our algorithm: suffix direct-MH SMC}

Our method runs independent SMC samplers indexed by $j=1,\ldots,J$. Run $j$
uses a cap $C_j$, a terminal bias $\lambda_j$, and its own bias schedule
\[
0=\lambda_{j,0}>\lambda_{j,1}>\cdots>
\lambda_{j,L_j}=\lambda_j.
\]
At level $k$, define
\[
\gamma_{j,k}(x)
=
\pM(x)e^{-\lambda_{j,k}\psi_{C_j}(x)},
\qquad
\pi_{j,k}(x)
=
\frac{\gamma_{j,k}(x)}{Z_{j,k}}.
\]

\subsection{One SMC run}

Generate $N_j$ initial particles independently from the base model,
\[
X_{j,0}^{(i)}\sim\pM,
\qquad
W_{j,0}^{(i)}=\frac{1}{N_j}.
\]

For $k=1,\ldots,L_j$, perform the following operations in order:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
    \item Apply $M$ suffix direct-MH transitions to every particle. These
    transitions leave $\pi_{j,k-1}$ invariant and use the acceptance
    probability
    \[
    \boxed{
    A_{j,k}^{\rm SMC}(x,y)
    =
    \min\left\{
    1,
    e^{-\lambda_{j,k-1}
    [\psi_{C_j}(y)-\psi_{C_j}(x)]}
    \right\}.}
    \]
    The suffix proposal is generated exactly as in TPS, except that its length
    may be drawn from a chosen distribution over the allowed suffix lengths.

    \item Move the weighted population from $\pi_{j,k-1}$ to $\pi_{j,k}$ with
    the incremental potential
    \[
    G_{j,k}(x)
    =
    \frac{\gamma_{j,k}(x)}{\gamma_{j,k-1}(x)}
    =
    e^{-(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{j,k-1})\psi_{C_j}(x)}.
    \]
    Thus
    \[
    \widetilde W_{j,k}^{(i)}
    =
    W_{j,k-1}^{(i)}G_{j,k}(X_{j,k}^{(i)}),
    \qquad
    W_{j,k}^{(i)}
    =
    \frac{\widetilde W_{j,k}^{(i)}}{
    \sum_r\widetilde W_{j,k}^{(r)}}.
    \]

    \item Compute
    \[
    \ESS(W_{j,k})
    =
    \frac{1}{\sum_i(W_{j,k}^{(i)})^2}.
    \]
    At an intermediate level, resample if the normalised ESS is below the
    chosen threshold, then reset all particle weights to $1/N_j$. At the final
    level, the default algorithm does not resample: it retains the normalised
    terminal weights $W_{j,i}=W_{j,L_j}^{(i)}$.
\end{enumerate}

Consequently, the final weighted empirical population
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N_j}W_{j,i}\,\delta_{X_{j,i}}
\]
approximates
\[
q_j(x)
=
\frac{
\pM(x)e^{-\lambda_j\psi_{C_j}(x)}
}{Z_j}.
\]

\subsection{Our estimator}

For every component $j$,
\[
\frac{\pM(x)}{q_j(x)}
=
Z_j e^{\lambda_j\psi_{C_j}(x)}.
\]
The unknown $Z_j$ cancels when numerator and denominator are normalised
separately within that component. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\bar f}_j
=
\frac{
\displaystyle
\sum_{i=1}^{N_j}
W_{j,i}e^{\lambda_j\psi_{C_j}(X_{j,i})}f(X_{j,i})
}{
\displaystyle
\sum_{i=1}^{N_j}
W_{j,i}e^{\lambda_j\psi_{C_j}(X_{j,i})}
}.}
\]
When $f=f_a$, we denote this separately normalised component estimator by
$\widehat F_j(a)=\widehat{\bar f}_j$.
For a tail probability,
\[
f(X_{j,i})
=
\1\{\phi_{\rm raw}(X_{j,i})\geq a\}.
\]
The cap appears only in the sampling correction; the event is always evaluated
using the raw observable.

Only after computing one self-normalised estimate for every component do we
combine them. With equal component coefficients,
\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\bar f}_{\rm SMC\text{-}SNIS}
=
\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\widehat{\bar f}_j.}
\]
More generally, chosen coefficients $\alpha_j>0$ with
$\sum_j\alpha_j=1$ give
\[
\widehat{\bar f}_{\rm SMC\text{-}SNIS}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\alpha_j\widehat{\bar f}_j.
\]
This is a mean of separately normalised component estimates. The samples from
different components are not pooled into one numerator and denominator, and
the estimator does not require the normalising constants $Z_j$.

\subsection{Optional final resampling}

If an unweighted terminal population is explicitly required, one may draw
$\bar X_{j,1},\ldots,\bar X_{j,N_j}$ by resampling the terminal particles with
probabilities $W_{j,1},\ldots,W_{j,N_j}$ and then set every weight to $1/N_j$.
The common factor $1/N_j$ cancels from the estimator, yielding the previously
used unweighted formula
\[
\widehat{\bar f}_j^{\rm res}
=
\frac{\sum_i e^{\lambda_j\psi_{C_j}(\bar X_{j,i})}f(\bar X_{j,i})}
{\sum_i e^{\lambda_j\psi_{C_j}(\bar X_{j,i})}}.
\]
This is a valid special case, but it introduces an additional random
resampling operation. Retaining $W_{j,i}$ is therefore the generic default and
avoids that extra source of Monte Carlo variance.

\section{Essential difference}

The original method generates annealed TPS trajectories, uses MBAR
normalising constants to correct the samples, and then combines all corrected
samples in one ratio. Our method transports particle populations with SMC,
retains the terminal SMC weights, computes one weighted self-normalised
importance-sampling ratio for each terminal population, and averages those
ratios only at the end. Final resampling remains an optional compatibility
mode when unweighted output particles are needed.

\section{Empirical comparison on the ARI experiment}

The current matched experiment uses TinyStories-8M, a fixed 100-token
completion, the public full-sequence ARI convention, and the prompt
\begin{quote}
\texttt{Once upon a time, in a big forest, there lived a rhinoc}.
\end{quote}
The IID reference contains 200,000 completions. One complete SMC seed group
contains nine independently run components, with 256 final particles per
component and different pairs $(C_j,\lambda_j)$. Table~\ref{tab:direct-smc}
reports the mean of the nine estimators $\widehat F_j(a)$.
These recorded runs used the optional final-resampling mode, so their terminal
weights were uniform and the unweighted special case above was applied. The
numbers remain valid for that implementation, but they can contain avoidable
final-resampling variance; they are not presented as measurements from the new
weighted-terminal default.

\begin{table}[h]

\hline
ARI threshold $a$ & IID direct & SMC--SNIS & relative difference \\
\hline
6 & $2.7460\times10^{-2}$ & $3.184786\times10^{-2}$ & $+16.0\%$ \\
7 & $5.1850\times10^{-3}$ & $5.089188\times10^{-3}$ & $-1.8\%$ \\
8 & $9.9500\times10^{-4}$ & $8.242966\times10^{-4}$ & $-17.2\%$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Matched direct and component-averaged SMC--SNIS estimates.}
\label{tab:direct-smc}

The mean corrected importance-sampling ESS divided by the number of component
samples was 0.180. Direct sampling generated 20,000,000 completion tokens in
1,119.8 seconds, whereas the nine SMC components generated 2,341,353 suffix
tokens in 1,160.6 seconds on the recorded Colab run. The lower token count did
not translate directly into lower wall time because SMC repeatedly evaluates
ARI, performs suffix mutation, and manages particle populations.

These numbers are preliminary. Only one complete seed group was available,
so between-seed variance of the final averaged estimator was not measurable.
The individual $\widehat F_j(a)$, corrected ESS, event counts, and reverse
normalising-constant diagnostics vary substantially across terminal biases;
they must be inspected before deciding which components are useful.

\subsection{What is currently known relative to TPS}

A previous one-seed experiment compared the sampling stages, without MBAR.
For the selected configurations, suffix SMC required 441.7 seconds and
annealed TPS required 3,881.1 seconds. Their final tilted mean ARI values were
12.516 and 14.050, respectively. SMC had final weight ESS divided by particle
count 0.496, while the TPS final-window temporal ESS was 9.17. The proposal
budgets were different, so this result demonstrates neither equal-compute
superiority nor probability-estimation accuracy.

Consequently, no numerical claim is currently made that SMC--SNIS estimates
base-model probabilities more accurately than TPS--MBAR. The accompanying
TPS--MBAR notebook now makes that missing experiment reproducible: it runs
independent annealed TPS chains, performs MBAR over all bias windows, and
reports unbiased-state tail estimates and overlap. A valid final comparison
must run both pipelines with the same model, prompt, completion length, ARI
implementation, thresholds, and clearly stated compute budget.

\section{Diagnostics and scope}

For SMC, target ESS and corrected importance ESS answer different questions.
The first diagnoses representation of the tilted target; the second diagnoses
overlap after correction to $\pM$. A strong tilt may discover rare outputs
while producing an unstable probability estimate. For TPS, temporal ESS,
between-chain agreement, burn-in sensitivity, and MBAR overlap are required.
Neither a large observed ARI nor completion of a finite run proves convergence.

The QwenGuard-Gen experiment uses the same SMC machinery with Unsafe log-odds
as $\phi$. It is included only to demonstrate that the observable is
replaceable. Guard scores and category labels are proxies and are not, by
themselves, calibrated probabilities of actionable harm.
